Keeping Your St. Petersburg Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in St. Petersburg

Around St. Petersburg, water heater trouble usually come from a handful of causes. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often used up, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Code is another piece. Replacing an older heater frequently means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the plumbing is a closed system, a T&P discharge line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Homes on well water have their own issues, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the rotten-egg smell that occasionally shows up when the water reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these can be fixed, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Call (727) 658-5497 and we'll take a look.
