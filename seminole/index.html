Keeping Your Seminole Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
