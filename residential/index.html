Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. So Largo Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the burner assembly or heating elements, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

The trouble with water heater failures is that most of them develop out of sight. Minerals build a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod gets eaten away a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one forces the issue. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Largo Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A tank in its later years gets a closer look than a new heater, a tankless unit on hard water needs annual descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a household of five cycles the heater far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your equipment and water, so every tune-up keeps the unit efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

The payoff shows up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust heats water more efficiently, a fresh anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of an early replacement, regular maintenance is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Largo Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the flow rate in GPM against the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Households change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can overwhelm the tank that once kept up just fine. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while hybrid heat pump tanks draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the real trade-offs in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Largo

A cold tap where hot water should be, a pilot that won't stay lit, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has its own cause and calls for its own repair. Largo Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is a warning that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where pressure is climbing, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
