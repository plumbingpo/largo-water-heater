Most gas water heaters in Largo, FL work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Largo Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
